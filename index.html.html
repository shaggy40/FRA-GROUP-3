India's largest retail NBFC. Pivoted from 2-wheeler finance to diversified consumer lending. Provides a sharp contrast to HDFC Bank — same macro risks, structurally different credit profile (unsecured, higher CC).

Why this combination?  The assignment prohibits combining options (a) and (b). We chose one large private bank (HDFC Bank, option a) and India's largest retail NBFC (Bajaj Finance, option d). This pairing makes the analysis analytically rich: the same macro shocks — COVID, IL&FS, demonetisation — hit both, but their funding structure (deposits vs market borrowings) and loan book (secured retail + corporate vs unsecured consumer) create meaningfully different NPA dynamics and market reactions.

Systematic vs Idiosyncratic Risk
What was economy-wide vs entity-specific — and what each event did to credit metrics.
Systematic Risk = Affects the entire sector simultaneously. Both entities (and peers) show correlated deterioration in NPAs / credit cost. Neither is unique to one lender.
Idiosyncratic Risk = Entity-specific. Peers are unaffected or move differently. Traceable to management decisions, product strategy, or regulatory action against one lender.
FY2017

Demonetisation — Agriculture & Rural NPA Stress

Systematic

November 2016 demonetisation disrupted rural cash flows. HDFC Bank's GNPA crossed 1% for the first time (0.9% → 1.1%). ICICI and Axis faced larger spikes from coinciding corporate loan stress. Bajaj Finance also saw consumer segment pressure, though at smaller scale. No bank escaped — the direction was common, the magnitude differed by portfolio mix.

FY2018–19

ICICI / Axis Corporate NPA Cycle — Infra & Power Sector Failures

Systematic (corporate lending) Idiosyncratic advantage: HDFC Bank

ICICI Bank GNPA peaked at 8.84% (FY18) and Axis Bank at 6.77% — driven by infrastructure/power/telecom corporate loan slippages. HDFC Bank peaked at only 1.4% (FY19). The divergence is partly systematic (sector had stressed infra loans) but partly idiosyncratic: HDFC Bank deliberately avoided large structured corporate credits that ICICI and Axis had aggressively built. BAF (retail-only book) was untouched by this corporate cycle — confirming the stress was not purely systematic.

FY2019

IL&FS Default — NBFC Liquidity Crunch

Systematic (NBFCs) Idiosyncratic sensitivity: Bajaj Finance

IL&FS default (Sep 2018) triggered an NBFC-wide liquidity crisis. Mutual funds and banks pulled short-term funding from NBFCs. Bajaj Finance's cost of funds rose sharply; growth slowed in H2 FY19. HDFC Bank (deposit-funded, access to RBI repo) was insulated. BAF's asset quality held (GNPA ~1.4%), demonstrating portfolio strength — but the funding side pressure was real and idiosyncratic to BAF's market-borrowing-dependent model.

FY2021

COVID-19 — Pandemic Shock (Moratorium, Provisioning)

Systematic — Both entities impacted

RBI granted 6-month moratorium (Mar–Aug 2020). HDFC Bank: Reported GNPA was 1.3% (masked by moratorium norms); credit cost spiked to ~1.0% as the bank provisioned preemptively. PCR held at ~70%. Bajaj Finance: At the COVID market crash (Mar-20), BAF GNPA stood at 1.61% — stress was latent, not yet visible. As moratorium expired, GNPA rose to 1.79% by FY21 year-end (Mar-21), then peaked at 2.96% in Q1 FY22 (Jun-21). Credit cost hit ~3.0% of AUM. Note: the equity market priced this in a year before the GNPA data confirmed it — price led the NPA data (see Share Price tab). Direction was systematic; magnitude was driven by book composition.

FY2021

RBI Credit Card Embargo — HDFC Bank Specific

Idiosyncratic — HDFC Bank only

RBI barred HDFC Bank from issuing new credit cards (Dec 2020–Aug 2021) following repeated IT/digital outages. This was entirely entity-specific — no peer faced this action. ICICI Bank and Axis Bank gained card market share during the embargo period. No direct credit quality impact (NPAs were unaffected), but significant revenue opportunity cost. The market treated it as a governance/operational risk event, not a credit risk event.

FY2024

HDFC–HDFC Ltd Merger — Balance Sheet Integration Stress

Idiosyncratic — HDFC Bank only

Merger effective July 1, 2023 (Q1 FY24). Advances grew 54.4% to ₹25.7 lakh crore (organic was ~15%); GNPA rose from 1.12% → 1.24% partly due to incorporating HDFC Ltd's mortgage NPA norms. Credit/deposit ratio jumped from 88.3x to 108x, compressing NIMs from ~4.3% to ~3.4%. No peer (ICICI, Axis, Kotak) was affected. This created a data break — FY24 HDFC Bank figures are not directly comparable to FY23.

FY2024

RBI eCOM / Insta EMI Lending Ban — Bajaj Finance

Idiosyncratic — Bajaj Finance only

November 2023: RBI directed BAF to immediately cease loan sanctions under its high-volume ‘eCOM’ and ‘Insta EMI’ channels for non-compliance with Key Fact Statement (KFS) lending guidelines. These channels accounted for a significant share of BAF’s consumer digital disbursements. Share price fell ~4% intraday on the news, then recovered fully to close +1.9% higher — the market correctly read it as a process/compliance issue rather than a credit quality event. The ban was lifted after BAF addressed the KFS gaps. No peer (Cholamandalam, Shriram, MuthootFin) faced similar regulatory action — this is the clearest idiosyncratic regulatory event in BAF’s recent history.

FY2025

Bajaj Finance Rural B2C & 2-Wheeler Book Stress

Idiosyncratic — Bajaj Finance only

BAF's aggressive push into rural B2C lending (FY22–24) began showing elevated delinquencies in FY25. BAF proactively cut this book by ~30%. The 2-wheeler/3-wheeler captive book (<1% AUM) contributed 13% of GNPA in Q4 FY26. Peers with different product mix (Shriram Finance in CV lending, Cholamandalam in vehicle/home) were not impacted in the same way. BAF is managing it out — GNPA already recovering to 1.01% by Q4 FY26.

Bajaj Finance — Share Price Events

COVID Crash (Jan–Mar 2020)~–53% in March 2020 alone

The market priced in NBFC-specific funding risk (echoing IL&FS anxiety) + unsecured consumer exposure before the asset quality deterioration was visible in reported numbers. Direction correlated; magnitude was idiosyncratic to BAF's risk profile.

COVID Recovery (Apr 2020–Dec 2021)~+280% from Mar 2020 lows
HDFC Bank recovery (same period)~+90%

BAF recovered far faster — market priced in rapid credit quality normalization and consumer credit demand recovery. BAF's growth NBFC premium (high AUM growth potential) reasserted itself once systemic risk lifted. This asymmetry (falls more, recovers more) is consistent with higher beta for growth NBFCs.

Data Notes & Methodology
How data breaks, definitional changes, and comparability issues were handled.

HDFC Bank — Data Breaks

Merger Break (July 1, 2023): HDFC Ltd merged into HDFC Bank. Q1 FY24 onwards, the bank absorbed HDFC Ltd's ₹7+ lakh crore mortgage book.
Impacts: (1) Advances +54.4% FY24 vs FY23 (organic ~15%); (2) C/D ratio: 88x → 108x; (3) GNPA: 1.12% → 1.24% — partly from HDFC Ltd's housing loan NPA ageing norms; (4) NIM: ~4.3% → ~3.4%.

Bajaj Finance — Data Breaks

Name & Model Change (2010): Bajaj Auto Finance Ltd became Bajaj Finance Ltd, simultaneously pivoting from 2-wheeler finance to diversified consumer lending. Pre-2010 data not comparable.

Within-window data break — Overdue Norm Migration (FY16–FY18): BAF's NPA recognition threshold was progressively tightened from 5 months overdue (FY16) → 4 months (FY17) → 3 months (FY18), aligning with RBI's 90-DPD standard. BAF itself cautions that GNPA figures across FY16–FY18 are not comparable on a like-for-like basis. The tightening mechanically inflated reported GNPA during this window even without genuine credit deterioration. ⚑ Charts mark this period with a caveat; the FY16 starting GNPA of 0.7% is likely understated relative to the 3-month norm applied from FY18 onward.

Handling: Analysis begins FY2016 — well past the 2010 pivot. The overdue norm migration is disclosed in the chart footnote and Data Notes. The FY16–FY18 trend should be read with caution; the FY19–FY24 series (3-month norm throughout) is fully comparable.
PCR Disclosure: BAF (as NBFC under IndAS/ECL framework) operates under IndAS / ECL provisioning rather than RBI's incurred-loss model, so PCR is not reported in the standard bank format. However, BAF does separately disclose Stage-3 provision coverage in its quarterly earnings presentations and investor presentations — this is the closest NBFC equivalent to bank PCR.
Handling: We use the proxy PCR ≈ 1 − (NNPA/GNPA). FY24: ≈ 1 − (0.37/0.85) ≈ 56.5%. BAF's reported Stage-3 coverage ratio (from earnings releases) is typically higher, as it reflects ECL lifetime provisions rather than incurred-loss specific provisions. Both metrics indicate adequate provisioning; the proxy is used here for cross-entity comparability. The comparison to bank PCR (74% for HDFC Bank) is not apples-to-apples and is so noted throughout.

Metric Definitions Applied

GNPALoans 90+ DPD (days past due) per RBI guidelines. Excludes moratorium-masked accounts during Mar–Aug 2020 for bank disclosures.
NNPAGNPA less specific provisions held against those accounts. Net of provisioning buffer.
PCR (Banks)Specific provisions / Gross NPA. HDFC Bank FY24 PCR of 74% is on specific provisions only; including floating/contingent = 195% (inflated by extraordinary floating provisions). We use 74% (specific only) throughout for conservative comparability.
Credit CostAnnualised provisions & contingencies / average net advances (banks) or average AUM (BAF). Not directly comparable between bank and NBFC.
Slippage RatioFresh NPA additions in the year / opening gross advances. BAF equivalent: gross loan loss to average AUM.
Write-offsLoans written off reduce GNPA stock but represent fully-provisioned realised losses. Recovery of written-off loans is upside, booked in "other income."
